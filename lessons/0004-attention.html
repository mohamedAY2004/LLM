Lesson 4 of 6 · Attention (2014–15) · about 20 minutes · the most important lesson

A new context vector for every word you write

Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio named the problem outright: "the use of a fixed-length vector is a bottleneck." Their fix underlies everything after it: Transformers, BERT and GPT. Once you can run it by hand, deck 03's Q/K/V slide is only new names for the same thing.

1 · Keep every encoder state

The encoder runs as before, but now all of its hidden states h₁…hn are handed to the decoder. Bahdanau et al. used a bidirectional RNN, one pass left→right and one right→left, and concatenated the two states at each position. As a result, "each annotation hi contains information about the whole input sequence with a strong focus on the parts surrounding the i-th word."Quoted from Bahdanau et al. 2014, §3. The paper calls the encoder states "annotations." This course calls them encoder hidden states. Think of hj as "word j, seen in context."

2 · Three steps at every decoder step

Before writing output word i, the decoder takes its current state si−1 and does this:

① score   eij = a( si−1, hj )   for every source position j How relevant is source word j to what I'm about to write? Bahdanau's a is a tiny neural net: va⊤ tanh(Wa si−1 + Ua hj), called "additive" attention. A plain dot product s·h also works and is faster (lab below).
② normalise   αij = exp(eij) / Σk exp(eik) Softmax: the weights are ≥ 0 and add up to 1 across the source positions.
③ blend   ci = Σj αij hj A context vector built fresh for output word i. The decoder uses ci, together with si−1 and the previous word, to produce si and the next word.

Compare with lesson 3: there was one c for the whole sentence. Now there is a ci for every output word, each a different mix of the same encoder states.The Transformer paper compares the two scoring functions: "dot-product attention is much faster and more space-efficient in practice, since it can be implemented using highly optimized matrix multiplication code" (Vaswani et al. 2017, §3.2.1).

3 · Run it yourself

Toy numbers, hand-picked so that you can read them. Each encoder state has 3 made-up features [noun "cat", colour, action]. Real models learn hundreds of unlabeled dimensions. The lab uses a dot-product score for clarity. Click each preset, then edit the query numbers and watch the weights move.

The heatmap at the bottom is the kind of figure in Bahdanau et al.'s paper (their Figure 3). Rows are output words and columns are source words. Notice that it is not a diagonal. The model is free to read the source in any order the target language needs.

4 · Four things the slides don't say

a. Nobody labels the alignments

The model is trained only to translate. The weights α are whatever makes translation work. Yet in the paper's analysis, "the (soft-)alignments found by the model agree well with our intuition." The alignment emerges on its own. It is not supervised.Bahdanau et al., abstract.

b. "Soft" is what makes it trainable

A hard choice ("read only hsat") has no gradient. A softmax-weighted blend is smooth, so backpropagation can adjust the scoring function along with the rest of the network. The paper's phrase is a "(soft-)search" over the source.

c. Why the bottleneck disappears

Every output step has a direct, one-step route to every source position, whatever the sentence length. In the paper's Figure 2, the attention model (RNNsearch-50) keeps its BLEU score on long sentences, while the plain encoder–decoder (RNNenc) falls off.

d. The weights are not parameters

The scoring function (Wa, Ua, va) is learned and fixed after training. The weights α are recomputed for every sentence and every output step. Mixing these two up is the most common misconception about attention.

5 · Rename it: query, key, value

Deck 03, slide 12 gives Q, K, V without saying where they come from. They come from here:

RoleDeck 03's descriptionIn Bahdanau attention
Querywhat this token is searching fordecoder state si−1
Keythe label describing what it offers (used to score)encoder state hj
Valuethe content actually passed on (what gets blended)encoder state hj, the same vector as the key
The bridge to lesson 5

In Bahdanau attention, keys and values are the same vectors, and the query comes from a different sequence (the decoder). The Transformer changes both: it gives keys and values separate learned versions, and it lets the query come from the same sentence. That second change is self-attention.

6 · What still breaks

The RNN underneath

Attention sits on top of RNNs. Each hj still waits for hj−1, and each si still waits for si−1. Training can't parallelise across positions: "thousands of GPU cores sit completely idle" (deck 03, slide 6). Inside the encoder, word 1 and word 30 still interact only through about 30 recurrent steps. The attention part works well. The recurrent part is what holds things back.

Without looking: write the three attention equations for decoder step i, give each a one-line meaning, then label which things are the query, the keys and the values.

Primary source for this lesson

Read the abstract and §3.1, and look at Figure 2 (BLEU vs sentence length) and Figure 3 (alignment heatmaps). It is short and readable, and it is where attention starts.
